The big insight. Every paper since 2022 has had the same six questions: column interaction diagram (always the biggest), shear design by the general method, Hillerborg strip method, flexural design of a T/L/rectangular beam, deemed-to-conform deflection and cracked-section service stress. The numbers change; the steps don't. One in-depth pass through each procedure, then two timed repetitions, will cover most of the marks.
Your AS 3600 must be unannotated, so you can't tab or write in it. Your note sheet has to tell you which clause or table to open for each step. The cheat sheet I made is built that way: every line ends with its clause number.

In the exam room

Spend the 180 minutes like this

  1. Planning time (10 min): read every question and write the "givens" next to each one: f′c → α₂, γ, α₁, Ec; bar areas; cover; stirrup size. Decide the order.
  2. Start with your strongest procedure, not Q1. For most people that's flexure (20–25 min) and service stress (20 min). They're quick, reliable marks.
  3. Budget about 1 minute per mark. The paper prints "ALLOW xx minutes" next to every question: respect it. Moving on at the time limit is worth more than finishing one question perfectly.
  4. Column (50 marks): do the parts in order; squash load and the strain/stress sketches are 10 easy marks even if the pure-bending iteration goes wrong.
  5. Shear (40 marks): the 2026 data sheet lists 12 steps. Number your working 1–12 to match, because the markers follow the sheet.
  6. Last 10 minutes: sketches (cross-section with bars + stirrups labelled, strip load diagrams with every dimension). They're 2–5 marks each and fast.
State every assumption and verify it. "Assume steel yields → ku = 0.06 ≤ 0.545 ✓", "Assume NA in flange → γkud = 30 mm ≤ 150 ✓", "Assume middle bars yield → ε = 0.0033 ≥ 0.0025 ✓". The marking schemes give a mark for each of these.
Learn

Eight lessons, explained from the physical picture up

Lesson 1 is the shared toolkit (loads, materials, effective depth, φ). Lessons 2–7 are the six exam questions. Lesson 8 is development length and curtailment, which feeds the shear question and appears in the 2026 quick-sheets. Each lesson ends with the past-paper question to do next.

Notation as on your data sheet. M* and V* are ULS design actions; M*s is the service moment; φ is the capacity reduction factor; ku d is the neutral-axis depth; γ ku d is the stress-block depth; α₂ f′c is the stress-block intensity. Units: N, mm, MPa, so moments are in N·mm (×10⁶ for kN·m).
Concept drill

The theory marks, and the decisions inside every calculation

The exam has no multiple choice, but every paper has 2–6 marks of "explain" (fundamental assumptions, strip vs simplified method, three ways to reduce deflection, effect of Asc, is the design still OK if the section changes). The rest of these questions are the decision points where students lose marks inside the long calculations: which width, which span, which sign, which φ.
